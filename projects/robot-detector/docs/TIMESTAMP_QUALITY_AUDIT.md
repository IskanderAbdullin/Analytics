# Timestamp collision and observed time-resolution audit

**Created:** 2026-11-19. **Module:** `src/RobotDetector.Core/TimestampQualityAudit.cs`. **Research-only** independent feature, no trading APIs, no order placement, no user accounts, no owner/parent-order identification.

## Problem being addressed

A market-data CSV may show several public trade prints with exactly the same exchange timestamp. This could be multiple fills, distinct orders, vendor-level time rounding, exchange batching or unrelated trades. It is **not valid** to silently deduplicate these prints based only on `TRADE_TS`, price, or reported side. Nor does the finest observed timestamp digit prove the underlying exchange clock's true precision.

T-Invest historical anonymized trade archives document `TRADE_TS,TICKER_CC,DIRECTION,PRICE,QUANTITY,TRADE_SOURCE,INSTRUMENT_UID`: https://developer.tbank.ru/invest/services/history-md/head-history-md .

Research on reconstructing order flow from aggregated prints illustrates the ambiguity of coincident time/price executions: https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.02759 . For comparison, Muravyev & Picard (2022) discuss how timestamps with 1ms precision enable subsecond market-clock studies in a different source/market: https://doi.org/10.1111/fima.12405 . Neither result authorizes identifying physical trader counts from this provider's public files.

## Deterministic, no-dedup methodology

For each instrument UID, trade source (EXCHANGE vs DEALER), trading mode and reported session, calculate:

- Number of original public prints, unique UTC timestamps, count of **distinct timestamp collision groups** (two or more prints share exact time), number of prints in such groups and largest group cardinality.
- Number of prints sharing both **exact timestamp and price** (still not necessarily duplicates).
- Number of timestamps aligned exactly to a whole second, 100ms, 10ms, 1ms; count with a submillisecond component; count not aligned to 1µs (DateTimeOffset has 100ns tick representation).
- Smallest positive interval between **distinct** recorded UTC timestamps in microseconds. It is descriptive, not an instrument's verified time resolution.
- Number of populated UTC minute blocks, observed collision/whole-ms shares, original file SHA, source feed-coverage flag.

All source public prints are retained unchanged. A simultaneous timestamp group of three prints counts as **three original trade records**, not one reconstructed order or three independent robots. Trade source, mode and session must never be mixed.

All results retain `OwnerCountKnown=false`, `SourceTimestampPrecisionVerified=false`, `StatisticalSignificanceEstablished=false`. An archival file does not establish that trade time was generated by the exchange rather than a downstream vendor, nor that the entire market-data feed is complete.

## Synthetic regression gates

1. Six original prints containing a 3-print same-time group, a 2-print same-time group and one isolated print; verify **all six** survive and only three unique timestamps are present.
2. Two price-identical prints inside each duplicate-time group must be **counted as public prints**, not collapsed to one inferred owner.
3. Distinguish exact 1ms, 500µs and 100ns precision in the time representation without claiming venue clock precision.
4. EXCHANGE/DEALER, trading modes and sessions are separately grouped even when original timestamps coincide.
5. Empty and single-print source groups are well defined; no false confidence or fabricated positive interarrival intervals.

### Significance for next stage

A **substantial fraction** of raw public prints have another public print at the exact same timestamp, often the same price. This is a risk for simplistic event-count, interarrival, FFT and periodic-size models. However, **same-time/same-price rows must not be deleted or treated as one parent order**, because the public archive does not supply the necessary source-order or economic event identifier.

A useful alternative is a separate **derived observable print-cluster view**, retaining every underlying source event ID, grouped conservatively by instrument/source/mode/session/exact UTC timestamp/price/reported direction, with aggregated lots and original print cardinality. It should be a separate analytical view and must never overwrite original history or be called an identifiable order/robot. Its structure can be compared to row-level original prints on synthetic known-truth cases and independent dates. See [STAGE_02_VALIDATION.md](STAGE_02_VALIDATION.md).

**Decision:** timestamp collision/precision data-quality gate PASSED as a deterministic descriptive audit; source clock provenance and true parent-order reconstruction are NOT verified.


## Interpretation and next gate

If many public prints share exactly the same timestamp and price, that is strong reason to audit print granularity and market venue semantics **before** modelling parent-order splits, iceberg replenishment or order counts. It is not itself evidence of fabrication or identifiable participant grouping. Any future deduplication must rely on **documented unique economic event identity/correction semantics**, not timestamp/price equality.

A high whole-millisecond or whole-second count might reflect true algorithmic clock-synchronized execution, timestamps being rounded by the provider, or exchange-level batching. Cross-check with independent provider data and market/session metadata before claiming a causal interpretation.
